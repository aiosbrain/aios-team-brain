What the packet does, and what its tests cover:

- **Aggregation before pagination.** SQL groups eligible, surviving, in-window messages by item, resolved member and UTC day, and the keyset predicate is applied to completed groups. The cursor's instant is never pushed into the message input, so an emitted group cannot reappear with a different count. Covered on real PostgreSQL: one thread spanning more groups than a page, more than `ITEM_LIMIT` stale and invisible items, a non-UTC session across a DST change, exact-microsecond ties, inclusive window bounds, and a group spanning many internal batches.
- **Order, cursor and window.** Order is `day DESC, at DESC, item ASC, member ASC`. The cursor is an opaque AES-256-GCM token with a fresh nonce and fixed associated data; it carries the complete binding and the last emitted tuple, at most 16 KiB. `asOf`, `since` and a fifteen-minute expiry are fixed by the first page and never slide. A tampered, re-keyed or oversized token is `invalid_request`; an expired one, or one presented with a different request, needs restart.
- **Provenance, credit and presentation.** Provenance is validated over the whole authorized Slack set before any candidate is limited. SQL candidates must equal the shared projector's groups in both directions for every loaded item, including an item loaded batches earlier, before the shared credit oracle filters them. Correction owner and lock are read by the packet's own session reader, never taken from a dependency. The credit-input digest and the presentation-input digest are separate bound values, each recomputed in a second, fresh validation transaction before publication.
- **Non-Slack dependencies.** A first page freezes the non-Slack days once. They may hold no Slack evidence, and their declared backing item IDs must cover every item the days visibly depend on — a packet-specific rule, not a general provenance policy. Those IDs must remain visible through the real membership oracle at publication and at final validation; removal needs restart, an unrelated addition does not.
- **Drain.** The drain returns exactly `{ window_days: 7, days }` only after verified terminal exhaustion and a fresh final validation. One `restart_required` discards the whole attempt and starts one fresh one; a second, or any other failure, throws. Budgets are shared by both attempts and nothing accumulated is ever returned on failure.
- **Deadlines and cleanup.** Every opaque loader and drain call receives an `AbortSignal` and runs under an injectable deadline. When it fires the signal is aborted, the transaction rolls back and the call rejects `budget_exhausted` without waiting for the abandoned work. Timers and listeners are cancelled on every terminal path, and an executor kept past its transaction is refused.
- **SQL timeout.** Both transactions are `REPEATABLE READ, READ ONLY`. The executor re-derives the transaction-local `statement_timeout` from the remaining budget before every statement, so a later statement does not inherit an earlier, larger bound.
- **Bounded output.** Page size is 1–512; candidates, rows, read bytes, serialized page bytes and elapsed time are budgeted, and exceeding any of them fails the whole page instead of truncating it. A compact aggregate is at most 2,048 bytes and carries one link message, not the group's message list.
